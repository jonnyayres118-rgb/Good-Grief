# Good Grief Plan Board Spec

## Goal

Make planning feel like assembling a reassuring set of useful building blocks, rather than completing a long writing exercise.

## Approved interaction model

- The planner home is a Plan Board made from five area stacks and twenty named blocks.
- Every block has one of three explicit states: Not started, Draft, or Sorted.
- Typing saves a draft but does not earn a badge.
- Pressing `Mark this sorted` places the block, earns its visible badge, and returns the user to the board.
- Editing a sorted answer returns that block to Draft until it is confirmed again.
- Progress is expressed as blocks in place, not a percentage-led score.
- Completing all four blocks in an area earns a named area badge.
- Prompt chips provide specific ways to begin, while an optional notes field retains flexibility.
- Status is never communicated through colour alone; every state includes readable text and an icon or shape.
- Motion is restrained and respects reduced-motion preferences.

## My plan

- Replace `Review & PDF` with `My plan`.
- `My plan` is a permanent, editable living summary grouped by the five product areas.
- It shows block status, the current answer, and sharing access controls.
- Remove PDF download controls, PDF creation code, generated PDF artifacts, dependencies, scripts, and all PDF-related product or membership copy.
- Membership value becomes the living plan, secure cross-device access, trusted sharing, and ongoing updates.

## Brand and product constraints

- Keep Montserrat throughout and retain the approved midnight navy, warm cream, acid yellow, hot pink, and blue palette.
- Product screens stay calmer than public campaign pages while remaining playful and life-affirming.
- Use sentence case and gentle copy. Death and grief are never the joke.
- Do not store passwords, PINs, card details, or authentication secrets.
- Preserve the five product areas, autosave, membership, billing, account creation, and permission-controlled sharing.
